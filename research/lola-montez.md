# Lola Montez

**Born:** Eliza Gilbert, Limerick, Ireland, 1818  
**Died:** Age 43, New York City (stroke)

*Source: © Jerome C. Krause*

---

> Famous (but bad) dancer; mistress to many, including Franz Liszt and Alexandre Dumas; acquaintance of Balzac, Victor Hugo, George Sand, Lamartine; intimate of kings and prime ministers; de facto ruler of Bavaria as King Ludwig I declined; belle of the California gold rush; would-be Queen of Lolaland. Early feminist of sorts and writer of a book of beauty tips. Homicidal temper; carried a whip. One of the most outrageous women of her time.

The Victorians delicately referred to her as *"La Grande Horizontale."*

---

## Early Life

Although Lola claimed to be an illegitimate daughter of the poet Lord Byron (and at other times, of a matador), her father was actually an 18-year-old British army lieutenant, and her mother a 13-year-old chorine. Her father died of cholera in India when Eliza was 7.

Her mother remarried, and Eliza was sent to Scotland, where she scandalized her stepfather's pious Presbyterian family by running naked through the streets. She was quickly sent to school in Paris, where she developed a gift for languages and honed her ferocious, violent temper to perfection.

At 19, she eloped back to Ireland, marrying a lieutenant named Thomas James — who soon ran off with a captain's wife.

---

## Inventing Lola Montez

She wanted to be an actress, but couldn't act. So with the backing of two admirers — Lord Malmesbury and Lord Brougham — Eliza Gilbert reinvented herself as **"Lola Montez," Spanish dancer**.

Her first London performance was dreadful (she couldn't dance either). The show closed to hisses, boos, and catcalls. In Lola's view, the audience simply lacked the sophistication to recognize her genius. As far as she was concerned, a star was born.

For several years she drifted about Europe taking whatever dancing engagements she could find. Her string of lovers grew — as did her temper. When a man annoyed her, she typically slashed him across the face with the whip she always carried. On one occasion she fired a pistol at a disappointing lover as he fled down the street, pants around his knees.

On first meeting, however, she was **charming. Very charming.**

*Whatever Lola wanted, Lola got.*

---

## Europe's Courts

**St. Petersburg** — Lola obtained a "private audience" with the Czar and received 1,000 rubles for services rendered.

**Dresden** — She captivated composer Franz Liszt, and the two enjoyed a burning passion. Jealous of the attention Liszt received from admirers, she burst into a royal banquet he was hosting and danced on the table among the dishes, spilling consommé into a duke's lap. Eventually Liszt — legendary lover of the age — was so worn out by Lola that he locked her in their hotel room while she slept and fled. He left money at the front desk for the furniture he knew she'd smash when she awoke.

**Warsaw** — The Viceroy of Poland offered her his finest country estate and mounds of diamonds to become his mistress. She found him physically repulsive and refused. He tried to have her fired mid-engagement; she announced his scheme to the audience from the stage. The audience rioted, spilling into the streets — to the great chagrin of the Viceroy (and his wife and other mistress).

**Greatest love** — Lola found her truest love in Alexandre Dujarier, an editor. He was promptly killed in a duel.

---

## Ruling Bavaria

Some months later, Lola resumed dancing in Munich. The theater manager fired her on the spot — her work was appallingly bad. Infuriated, Lola went directly to the royal palace, still in costume, and charged into **King Ludwig I's** private study demanding "justice."

The flustered King awkwardly inquired whether her figure was a work of nature or of art. Finding herself in control, Lola snatched scissors from his desk and slit the front of her dress to the waist. She left the palace with a substantial engagement at the Munich Theater. The manager was fired.

Ludwig fell desperately in love. He built her a palace with a marble fountain that sprayed perfumed water. He gave her an allowance from the public treasury. As his powers faded, **Lola effectively ruled Bavaria**, taking exuberant control of policy — harassing the Jesuits, introducing Napoleonic law. Her rule was strikingly liberal, alarming conservative Europe's power broker, Prince Metternich.

Metternich offered Lola $250,000 (equivalent) to go away quietly. She threw it in his emissary's face.

Metternich organized student riots against her. Lola had Ludwig close down the university. The students rioted again, joined by thousands of tradespeople — the whole town was under siege. Ludwig declared, *"I will never abandon Lola... My crown for Lola."* It cost him exactly that. He was forced to abdicate. Lola caught the midnight train out of town — a victim of her own political incompetence.

---

## California & the Gold Rush

At 35, after severe episodes of sickness and marriage, Lola needed a fresh start. She set off for California.

*"The ruffian miners appealed to her sensibilities and she felt that she belonged."*

She opened a **frontier saloon in Grass Valley**, outfitting it with:
- Louis XVI cabinets and ormolu mirrors
- Ludwig's jewels
- Kanaka houseboys
- A pet bear
- A swan bed
- Gold leaf everywhere
- One extra-large deep-red-top billiard table with dragons carved on its legs
- Every Governor, Senator, or millionaire she could haul through the door

The nightly show had one act: Lola at her loosest. It was, at last, a hit.

Letters discovered after her death revealed this was all in service of a plot: **Lola intended to "capture" California from the USA, cause it to declare independence, and crown herself Queen of "Lolaland."**

---

## Decline

Weirder delusions followed. Lola faded into mysticism and a bizarre form of astrology. She did revive briefly to write a book of beauty secrets. (To prevent wrinkles, she recommended tightly binding thin strips of raw beef all about the face — except the eyes — until all the "vibrant energy" had soaked in. No mention of how long that might take.)

She went on a lecture tour: *"Let historical justice be done to the intellect of woman... I am content to leave the history of her heart and moral life, without comment, to defend itself by contrast with that of the other sex."* She didn't give many lectures.

At 41, she suffered a schizophrenic collapse, abandoned the West, and spent her final two years on the streets of New York as a pauper — shuffling along, speaking aloud to herself, urging God to forgive her wicked life.

**At 43, she died of a stroke in a wretched boardinghouse, alone.**

Her two children declined to claim the body. Both were "constrained by the pressures of business" — one ran a lamp shade store in California, the other was in jail.

---

## Connection to the Gold Rush Trip

Lola Montez operated her famous saloon in **Grass Valley**, near the Malakoff Diggins area, during the peak of the Gold Rush. She is one of the "local color" characters represented in the Salmon Creek School 4th grade living history program — portrayed at the saloon during the Thursday town tour.
